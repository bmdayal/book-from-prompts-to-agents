# Inbox test results (fresh no-tools sessions, 24 Oct 2026)
## Round 1: inbox with explicit deadlines (9 emails)
- Vague prompt, 4 sessions: all four 3/3. Each put Dana (reply by 11:30 today) and Priya (signed copy by Wednesday, despite "no rush") ahead of the rest, and none called the "URGENT!!!" promo urgent. Section headings differed from run to run (Urgent / Time-bound / Small actions / Ignore, etc.).
- Detailed prompt (role, who matters, definition of urgent, output format), 4 sessions: all four 3/3. All used exactly the two requested sections. Two of the four put Sam's lunch reply under "Needs action" with a "low priority" note.
- So the chapter's original story ("completely missed the point") did NOT reproduce on this inbox. A vague prompt was fine when the emails state their own deadlines.
## Round 2: urgency depends on who sent it (6 emails; Marcus Chen's email never says he is the CEO and gives no deadline)
- Vague prompt, 4 sessions: all four said nothing was truly urgent. Marcus was never first: second in all four lists (first was Alex the recruiter in two runs, Taylor the reference-requester in two runs).
- Same task with the context "Marcus is our CEO, and anything from him goes to the top of my list" plus format, 4 sessions: Marcus was first in all four.
- Headline metric (pre-registered clause "E1 first in the action list"): vague 0 of 4, with context 4 of 4.
- Scoring note: my 0-3 rubric also scored the recruiter and the reference request as "not in the top group". Once the output format had only two sections ("Needs action" / "FYI"), that clause was ambiguous, because 2-3 of the four context runs put the reference request under "Needs action". I judged that ambiguity after seeing the replies, so I report only the Marcus-first result as the headline. The context did not make the AI sort the lesser items consistently.
## Caveats
Made-up inbox, one model, small samples. The reported replies are what each session returned as its final message.
